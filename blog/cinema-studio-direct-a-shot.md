Cinema Studio is its own workspace (top bar → **Cinema Studio**). It has a sidebar with **Home**, **My elements**, **My favorites**, **Community** and your **Projects**.

## The five setting cards

Above the prompt box sit five cards. Each one adds precise wording to your prompt, so you get the look without writing it.

| Card | What it controls | Try |
|---|---|---|
| **References** | Images of characters, locations and props | Add your hero and the room they stand in |
| **Film setup** | Genre, era and tempo of the edit | Noir, 1980s, Calm |
| **Camera** | Camera body, lens, aperture, plus moves | 35mm film, anamorphic, f/1.4, dolly in |
| **Color palette** | The grade the shot follows | Teal night, warm tungsten |
| **Lighting** | The lighting scheme | Practicals, window light, silhouette |

Leave any card on **Auto** and the model decides.

## Camera moves stack

Open **Camera → Movement** and press a move. It lands in your prompt as `#Dolly in`. Add several and they play in order: `#Crane up #Pan left`.

## Use elements, don't describe faces

Type `@` in the prompt to pick a saved element (`@Maya`, `@Diner`). Cinema Studio attaches its reference image, so the same person shows up shot after shot. See *Keep a character consistent with Elements and @mentions*.

## Image or video

The **Image / Video** switch on the left of the composer decides whether you get a still (Nano Banana 2.1) or a clip (Seedance 2.5). Stills are cheap: block out the shot as an image first, then switch to Video and use it as the start frame.

## Projects

Press **New project** in the sidebar before you start. Everything you generate while a project is open is filed under it, so one film stays in one place.
